The part nobody explains at the appointment

Why Hashimoto’s happens in the first place.

Five steps. Once you can see them, the food stops looking like a fad and starts looking like the obvious move.

01

You inherit the possibility, not the disease

Certain immune genes make this more likely. That part is fixed — and it is also not a sentence. Plenty of people carry the genes and never get sick. Genes load the gun. They do not pull the trigger.

02

Something opens the gut wall

The lining of the intestine is one cell thick, with junctions between the cells that are supposed to stay shut. Gluten, alcohol, seed oils, chronic stress, infections and some medications loosen them. Food proteins that should have stayed in the gut start reaching the bloodstream, where the immune system has never met them before.

03

The immune system mistakes your thyroid for the food

This is the step that explains everything else. Gliadin — the protein in gluten — has a shape strikingly similar to thyroid tissue. Casein, in dairy, is a frequent second offender. The immune system builds a weapon against the food, then finds the same shape on your thyroid and attacks that too. It is called molecular mimicry, and it is why removing the food matters more than any supplement.

04

The attack itself burns through minerals

An immune system running hot is expensive. Inflammation consumes selenium and zinc and locks iron away where you cannot use it. Meanwhile the damaged gut absorbs less of everything. You end up short of the exact minerals the thyroid needs — which makes the symptoms worse, which gets read as the gland failing rather than the tank being empty.

05

The thyroid only does about a fifth of the job

The gland mostly makes T4, which is storage. The active hormone is T3, and roughly 80% of it is converted elsewhere — mainly liver and kidney — by enzymes that need selenium, and processes that need zinc, iron, magnesium and B vitamins. Thyroid hormone replacement can look correct on paper and still leave someone exhausted if conversion is short of minerals.

The attack burns minerals. The damaged gut absorbs fewer. So you end up short of the exact things the thyroid needs — and that gets read as the gland failing.

Minerals the thyroid actually uses

Why a meat-based diet fits Hashimoto’s.

Inflammation spends selenium, zinc, iron, and magnesium. A damaged gut absorbs less. Beef, lamb, fish, and eggs put those minerals back in the form the body absorbs best.

Se

Selenium

The thyroid holds more selenium per gram than any other organ. The enzymes that turn T4 into T3 need it. 200 mcg dropped antibodies about 40% in trials. Do not megadose. Brazil nuts vary too much nut to nut — a capsule is cleaner.

Mg

Magnesium

Low-carb eating increases magnesium loss in urine. Low magnesium means cramps, poor sleep, and worse blood sugar. Thyroid patients are often low. Use 200–400 mg glycinate at night. NIH magnesium fact sheet.

Fe

Iron (heme)

Needed to build thyroid hormone and convert T4 to T3. Low ferritin is why labs can look “treated” while energy is still gone. Red meat is heme iron — absorbed far better than plant iron. Get a full iron panel before taking iron pills.

Zn

Zinc

Needed by the same conversion enzymes. Also helps the immune system tell self from not-self. Red meat, shellfish, and organ meat are the densest sources.

B12

B12

Thyroid patients are often low — gut damage and overlap with pernicious anaemia. Meaningful B12 is in animal food, almost nowhere else. Fatigue and brain fog track this number.

D

Vitamin D

Deficiency is common in Hashimoto’s and tied to higher antibodies. Dose to a blood level, not a guess. Recheck at week 8. Pair D3 with K2.

The one gap food cannot close

And the backstop, in case your body cannot use the cheap version.

Eating this way refills most of the tank on its own — that is the point of meals built from the densest mineral foods. A methylated multivitamin covers the gap food cannot close by itself.

Here is the specific problem it solves. A large share of people carry a variant in a gene called MTHFR, which means they convert folic acid — the synthetic form in cheap multivitamins and enriched bread — poorly or barely at all. If you cannot convert it, it does not just fail to help. Unconverted folic acid builds up, the methylation cycle it was meant to feed stalls, homocysteine climbs — and high homocysteine is itself inflammatory. The last thing an immune system already attacking your thyroid needs.

The fix is not a bigger dose. It is the right form. Methylfolate and methyl-B12 arrive already in the shape the body uses, so the gene variant stops mattering. That cycle then does three jobs at once: clears homocysteine, builds the raw materials for serotonin and dopamine, and makes glutathione — your own antioxidant, the one protecting mitochondria while the inflammation settles.

The scoreboard is homocysteine on a blood test, not a gene-test result. Test it, take the methylated forms for 90 days, test again. If the multi makes you feel wired, take it every other day.

Rheumatoid arthritis — the trial that proved food is the trigger

Kjeldsen-Kragh, The Lancet, 1991. 27 diet vs 26 control, randomized, one year. A 7–10 day fast, then foods added back one at a time to find what flared each person. At four weeks the diet group improved on tender joints, swollen joints, pain, morning stiffness, grip strength, ESR and CRP. Controls improved on pain only. The advantage held at one year across every measure.

The maintenance diet in this trial was gluten-free vegan, then lactovegetarian. What it establishes is the method — fast, remove, reintroduce one food at a time, keep out what flares. Carnivore is the same move from the opposite starting list; the two have never been run against each other.

What each trial actually tested

The Crohn’s trials used a defined exclusion diet that still includes rice, potato and some fruit. The psoriatic arthritis crossover had 16 completers, with both arms calorie-matched. The MS trial compared two elimination diets against each other rather than against an ordinary diet. Across these conditions the evidence for elimination is trial-grade; the evidence for meat-only specifically is case series and one large survey.

What falls, then what rises

Trigger foods leave the diet. CRP fell 29% in 10 weeks on AIP. Less inflammation means cells can make energy again. Insulin drops. Body fat — which itself drives inflammation — starts to shrink. T4 converts to T3 when selenium, zinc, iron, magnesium, and B vitamins are available.

The methylated multi · not a vitamin ad

Folic acid is the wrong key for a lot of locks.

Grocery multis use folic acid. Many people convert it poorly (MTHFR). Methylfolate + methyl-B12 are the forms the body actually uses. That is how you clear homocysteine, build serotonin/dopamine, and make glutathione — the antioxidant that protects mitochondria. If the multi makes you wired, take it every other day. Homocysteine is the scoreboard.

What success usually looks like

You will feel it before the lab printout does.

Do not judge the 90 days by TPO in week 2. Judge week 2 by bloating, cravings, sleep, and clothes. The papers above match this order.

Days 1–7

Salt, fat, sleep

Headache or “keto flu” if salt is low. Cravings drop when snack food is gone. This is the refill, not the crash. Eat until full. Fatty cuts only.

Days 8–21

The Esposito window

This is when that trial saw antibodies fall 44–57%. You may notice less puffiness, a quieter gut, a clearer head. Take a waist photo. Do not quit here.

Days 22–60

Weight and energy

Insulin is down. Fat tissue — an immune organ — starts to shrink. Walk after meals if you can. If energy tanks, you are too lean / too little food. Add fat.

Days 61–90

The second blood draw

Repeat TSH, free T3/T4, TPO, ferritin, D, homocysteine. Antibodies may still be falling. Feeling better with a quieter attack is a win even if TPO is not zero.

The 90-day diet

What to eat. What to skip. What to take.

Week 0

Labs. Photos. Waist. 1–10 scores. Buy food + 4 supplements. Ask a doctor to draw blood, not to approve the menu.

Days 1–30

Beef, lamb, fatty fish, salt, water. Eggs if they sit well. Butter. No gluten, cheese, plants, alcohol, “keto snacks.” Eat until full.

Days 31–90

Same food. Labs at week 8–12. Bloating and cravings move first. Antibodies can take the whole 90 days.

After 90

Stay, or add one food every 5–7 days. Never gluten first.

Breakfast or skip

Eggs + leftover steak. Or salted coffee if 16:8 has started.

Lunch

80/20 ground beef, smashed, salted. Or sardines.

Dinner

Ribeye, roast, lamb, salmon. Bone broth if something warm helps.
